\section{Projection-Relative Saturation}

\subsection{Why saturation must be projection-relative}

The finite mechanics contains several distinct forgetful maps.
Consequently, the statement that a system has ``lost information''
is incomplete unless the relevant map is named.

Let
\[
q:X\longrightarrow Y
\]
be a declared projection.

A distinction between \(x,x'\in X\) survives the projection when
\[
q(x)\neq q(x').
\]
It is hidden by the projection when
\[
x\neq x',
\qquad
q(x)=q(x').
\]

The second relation does not imply that the complete states have
merged.  It says only that the declared projection no longer resolves
their difference.

\begin{definition}[Projection-relative degeneracy]
Two distinct complete states \(x,x'\in X\) are projection-degenerate
relative to \(q\) when
\[
q(x)=q(x').
\]
\end{definition}

This is an exact statement about a map.

It is not an ontological identification of \(x\) and \(x'\).

\subsection{Independent registered distinction}

Let
\[
\mathcal X\subseteq X
\]
be a declared family of complete states.

The projected state set is
\[
q(\mathcal X)\subseteq Y.
\]

When the relevant notion of independence is cardinal, the number of
projected distinction classes is
\[
K_q(\mathcal X)
=
|q(\mathcal X)|.
\]

More generally, when a declared linear or combinatorial rank function
is available, write
\[
r_q(\mathcal X)
\]
for the independent distinction rank that survives the projection.

No universal rank function is assumed.

The choice must be stated for the particular register being studied.

\subsection{Native relational loading}

The registered carrier language supplies a native loading order
without introducing an external scalar parameter.

Let \(p\) be a live registered prefix and define
\[
\mathcal C(p)
\]
to be the set of accepted complete registered words extending \(p\).

If \(p'\) extends \(p\), then
\[
\mathcal C(p')\subseteq\mathcal C(p).
\]

\begin{definition}[Native relational loading]
Native relational loading is the accumulation of admitted relational
constraint under prefix extension, ordered by reverse inclusion of
the lawful completion family
\[
\mathcal C(p).
\]
\end{definition}

For the certified carrier language,
\[
|\mathcal C(p)|
:
8\longrightarrow2\longrightarrow1
\longrightarrow1\longrightarrow1.
\]

The first admitted directed edge reduces eight lawful registered
futures to two.

The second reduces two to one.

Later admitted extension propagates the already unique completion.

\subsection{Registered-future saturation}

\begin{definition}[Registered-future saturation]
A live prefix \(p\) is registered-future saturated when
\[
|\mathcal C(p)|=1.
\]
\end{definition}

This is an exact finite notion.

It does not mean that the complete relational state has become
unique.

At the two-edge threshold there are \(120\) distinct live contexts
and \(120\) distinct registered completed words.

Thus
\[
\text{unique lawful future}
\neq
\text{unique complete context}.
\]

The terminal completion register retains further structure: above
each of the \(15\) closing chambers there remain two native holonomy
designations.

Therefore native saturation is a loss of future freedom, not an
erasure of complete relational distinction.

\begin{theorem}[Native finite relational saturation]
Within the certified registered carrier language, admissible prefix
extension monotonically contracts the lawful completion family as
\[
8\longrightarrow2\longrightarrow1.
\]
Registered-future saturation occurs after two directed edges.
Complete relational distinction remains nontrivial at that threshold
and in the terminal completion register.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Five-address projection-relative saturation}

The native five-address registration gives a second exact form of
projection-relative saturation.

Let
\[
\rho:\mathbb R^{15}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^5
\]
sum the three sector coordinates belonging to each native
five-address fibre.

The centered fifteen-dimensional register decomposes into a
four-dimensional fibre-constant visible sector and a ten-dimensional
fibre-internal hidden sector.

Let
\[
H
\]
denote the projector onto that ten-dimensional retained sector.

Distinct centered states can therefore have the same five-address
readout while remaining distinct in the complete fifteen-state
register.

This is exact projection-relative saturation:
registration loses resolving power while complete distinction
survives.

\subsection{Context activation and retained distinction}

The native designation mechanism identifies the retained sector
exactly.

\[
D:=T_b-T_{ab}.
\]

\[

Let
\[

The coarse five-address registration sees none of it.

\subsection{Contrast saturation is a distinct operation}

Paper 14 also defines the sector contrast fraction
\[
\zetaRel(x)
=
\frac{\|\Pperp x\|_2^2}{\|x\|_2^2},
\qquad
x\neq0.
\]

A sequence is contrast-saturating when this quantity approaches a
stable minimum while the corresponding complete state sequence
remains nontrivial.

For normalized quadratic aggregation,
\[
\widehat Q=\frac1{98}Q,
\]
if
\[
\Pzero x\neq0,
\]
then
\[
\zetaRel(\widehat Q^{\,k}x)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus \(\widehat Q\)-iteration supplies an exact algebraic example of
contrast saturation.

But this is not the native loading operation.

Under the canonical completion-occupancy register, the native
\(8\to2\to1\) loading sequence has the exact stage values
\[
\zeta:
\frac{31}{55}
\longrightarrow
\frac{41}{65}
\longrightarrow
\frac{7}{10}.
\]

Every strict native loading extension increases contrast.

Therefore
\[
\text{native future saturation}
\neq
\text{contrast saturation}.
\]

Native loading sharpens a selected future.

Normalized quadratic aggregation suppresses centered contrast relative
to common mode.

Both are theorem-level finite operations, but they are distinct.

\subsection{Finite-resolution contrast saturation}

Exact arithmetic is not the only possible declared registration.

Let
\[
\varepsilon>0
\]
be a fixed resolution threshold, and let
\[
d=x-y
\]
be a nonzero centered distinction.

The contrast-concentration theorem gives
\[
\|\widehat Q^{\,k}d\|_2
\le
\left(\frac9{49}\right)^k
\|d\|_2.
\]

Therefore for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists a finite
\(k_\varepsilon\) such that
\[
0<
\|\widehat Q^{\,k}d\|_2
<
\varepsilon
\]
for all
\[
k\ge k_\varepsilon.
\]

The distinction remains mathematically nonzero, but a registration
with resolution \(\varepsilon\) no longer distinguishes it.

This is
\emph{effective registration degeneracy}.

It remains distinct from exact equality.

\subsection{Saturation is not global incompatibility}

Relational saturation is defined on an admitted finite carrier or
registered-action system.

It does not require that every stronger proposed transport extension
already be globally closed.

We distinguish:

\begin{description}

\item[Local inadmissibility.]
A proposed local transition is not contained in the admitted local
transport vocabulary.

\item[Global incompatibility.]
A specified family of locally admissible transitions cannot be
assembled into a stronger globally closed transport assignment under
its declared compatibility conditions.

\item[Relational saturation.]
Within an already admitted finite carrier or registration, lawful
future freedom or projected resolving power saturates while complete
relational distinction remains nontrivial.

\end{description}

The third must never be inferred from the second.

Conversely, an unresolved stronger global construction does not reopen
a saturation theorem already proved on a separately certified admitted
carrier.

\subsection{Boundary}

The exact finite theorem now contains two distinct saturation notions.

Native registered-future saturation is certified by
\[
8\longrightarrow2\longrightarrow1
\]
with complete contextual distinction retained.

Quadratic contrast saturation is certified by
\[
\zetaRel(\widehat Q^{\,k}x)\longrightarrow0
\]
for states with nonzero common component, while every finite iterate
remains invertible.

These notions are related through the finite response algebra and the
retained-sector identity
\[
\Delta^{\mathsf T}\Delta=\frac34H.
\]

They are not the same process.

No physical interpretation follows without an additional physical
bridge.
