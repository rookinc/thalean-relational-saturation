\section{Global Compatibility and the Inter-Face Sigma Frontier}

\subsection{Local law and global closure are distinct}

The current Project 41 seam construction supplies a locally admissible
inter-face transport vocabulary.

Within that construction, the local \(r_3\) action has a universal
form: it reverses side and polarity while preserving sheet. Its
section routing contains both same-section and changed-section
branches.

The completed local vocabulary is also closed under the independently
derived neighbor-frame involution \(E\).

These are local and covariance results.

They do not by themselves construct a globally consistent seam
transport.

The remaining global requirement is that the selected seam actions
satisfy the declared four-step closure relation
\[
(r_2r_3)^4=1
\]
everywhere in the complete flag system.

Thus the architecture separates

\[
\text{local admissibility}
\]
from
\[
\text{global compatibility}.
\]

\subsection{The inter-face sigma}

\begin{definition}[Inter-face sigma]
An inter-face sigma is a globally selected assignment of locally
admissible seam transport relations across adjacent faces satisfying
all declared covariance and closure conditions.
\end{definition}

The sigma is therefore not one additional long-range selector placed
on top of the local mechanics.

It is the global compatibility relation that determines which local
transport contributions can coexist in one admitted transport
structure.

Schematically,
\[
\text{local seam vocabulary}
\longrightarrow
\text{covariant compatibility}
\longrightarrow
\sigma
\longrightarrow
\text{admitted global transport}.
\]

\subsection{Completed E-closed seam vocabulary}

Neighbor-frame covariance enlarges the local admissible vocabulary.

After canonical completion under the involution \(E\), the global
search used
\[
2816
\]
distinct local seam options.

This completed vocabulary is the correct search domain for the present
global \(r_3\) problem.

The enlargement is mathematically important.

It shows that the earlier truncated vocabulary did not exhaust the
locally admissible seam possibilities once neighbor-frame covariance
was imposed.

But a larger locally admissible vocabulary does not imply global
closure.

\subsection{What the search establishes}

The Mac work supports the following internal conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

\item local admissibility and global compatibility are distinct;

\item neighbor-frame covariance changes the admissible local search
space;

\item substantial local admissibility does not guarantee global
closure;

\item the unresolved global object is an inter-face transport
relation rather than an independent long-range selector;

\item the remaining obstruction is global compatibility, not the
absence of a local seam law.

\end{enumerate}

These conclusions constrain the architecture of the saturation
conjecture.

They do not establish saturation itself.

\subsection{Sigma and the summation domain}

Section 9 argued that QR long-range structure is sought through lawful
summation of finite contributions.

The present section sharpens that statement.

The sum cannot be taken over arbitrary locally admissible terms.

The admissible summation domain must be compatible with the global
transport relation.

Thus a more precise schematic chain is
\[
\text{local contribution}
\longrightarrow
\sigma
\longrightarrow
\text{admitted summation domain}
\longrightarrow
\text{registered aggregate}.
\]

This is the role of sigma in the present conjectural architecture.

It does not create a new long-range force carrier.

It determines which local contributions belong to one globally
compatible relational history.

No theorem in this paper identifies unresolved sigma circulation with:

\subsection{Compatibility failure is not saturation}

The distinction is decisive for Paper 14.

A global transport assignment may fail because no collection of
locally admissible seam choices satisfies all closure constraints.

That is global incompatibility.

Relational saturation, by contrast, concerns an already admitted
globally compatible construction whose complete distinctions exceed
or outpace the resolving capacity of a declared registration.

Therefore
\[
\text{global incompatibility}
\neq
\text{relational saturation}.
\]

A failed closure search cannot be reinterpreted after the fact as
evidence that the system has saturated.

\subsection{The saturation prerequisite}

The Thalean Relational Saturation Conjecture therefore carries the
following prerequisite:

\begin{quote}
Saturation is defined only on an admitted globally compatible
transport structure.
\end{quote}

Equivalently:

\begin{quote}
Before anything can saturate globally, local transport must first
close globally.
\end{quote}
