\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The theorem in one sentence}

The central result of Paper 14 is:

\begin{quote}
The normalized Thalean quadratic preserves the common sector mode,
contracts every centered sector mode, remains invertible at every
finite iterate, and converges to the rank-one common-mode projection.
\end{quote}

This gives an exact finite example of

\[
\text{strong aggregate}
\]
together with
\[
\text{weak individuation}.
\]

The phrase is not metaphorical at the theorem level.

It refers to a precise spectral separation.

\subsection{Common mode and centered contrast}

The normalized operator is

\[
\widehat Q
=
\frac{1}{98}Q.
\]

Its common-mode eigenvalue is

\[
1.
\]

Its centered eigenvalues are

\[
\frac{9}{49},
\qquad
\frac{3}{98},
\qquad
\frac{1}{49}.
\]

Hence for every sector state \(x\),

\[
\widehat Q^{\,k}x
=
P_0x
+
\widehat Q^{\,k}P_\perp x,
\]

with

\[
\left\|
\widehat Q^{\,k}P_\perp x
\right\|_2
\le
\left(\frac{9}{49}\right)^k
\|P_\perp x\|_2.
\]

The common component persists.

The centered component becomes exponentially small.

\subsection{No transfer into the common mode}

It is important not to misread this result.

The common and centered subspaces are invariant under \(Q\).

Therefore the theorem does not describe centered amplitude being
converted into common-mode amplitude.

If

\[
P_0x=0,
\]

then

\[
P_0\widehat Q^{\,k}x=0
\]

for every \(k\).

A purely centered input remains centered and contracts toward zero.

Likewise, a common component that is initially present remains
unchanged under the normalized operator.

The concentration theorem is therefore a statement about differential
spectral persistence, not cross-eigenspace transfer.

\subsection{Why normalization is useful}

The unnormalized quadratic satisfies

\[
C(Q^kx)
=
98^kC(x)
\]

and

\[
R(Q^kx)
\le
18^kR(x).
\]

Both components can therefore grow in absolute magnitude.

Nevertheless,

\[
\frac{R(Q^kx)}{C(Q^kx)}
\le
\left(\frac{9}{49}\right)^k
\frac{R(x)}{C(x)}
\]

whenever \(C(x)\neq0\).

Thus the contrast-concentration phenomenon is already present under
raw \(Q\)-iteration.

Normalization by \(98\) simply removes the common exponential scale
and exposes the relative contraction directly.

This is why the natural saturation variable is not raw magnitude.

It is relative contrast.

\subsection{Invertible stages and a noninvertible limit}

A second important feature is

\[
\det(\widehat Q)\neq0.
\]

Hence

\[
\widehat Q^{\,k}
\]

is invertible for every finite \(k\).

But

\[
\widehat Q^{\,k}
\longrightarrow
P_0,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{rank}(P_0)=1.
\]

Thus Paper 14 exhibits a sequence of invertible finite-dimensional
operators converging to a noninvertible operator.

There is no contradiction.

The inverse becomes increasingly sensitive on the contracting
centered directions.

In exact arithmetic, finite-stage distinction remains recoverable.

At finite resolution, the same distinction may become effectively
invisible.

\subsection{Exact and effective degeneracy}

This suggests a useful distinction.

\begin{description}

\item[Exact projection degeneracy.]
Two complete states become identical under a declared many-to-one
projection.

\item[Effective registration degeneracy.]
Two complete states remain mathematically distinct, but their
registered difference falls below a declared resolution threshold.

\end{description}

The earlier finite mechanics already supplies exact examples of the
first type.

Paper 14 supplies an exact mechanism producing the second type.

A complete theory of relational saturation may require both.

\subsection{Projection loss and retained history}

The earlier theorem chain establishes finite constructions in which a
coarser projection returns while a more resolved construction retains
a receipt.

Examples include:

\begin{itemize}

\item lifted versus projected cycle return;

\item binary versus integer refinement;

\item quotient versus complete registered recurrence;

\item visible registration closure with retained history winding.

\end{itemize}

Paper 14 does not yet prove that these retained receipts coexist with
quadratic contrast concentration inside one admitted native loading
sequence.

That missing diagram is important.

The two sides currently exist as independently established structures:

\[
\text{contrast concentration}
\]

and

\[
\text{retained higher-resolution distinction}.
\]

The full native saturation theorem would join them.

\subsection{The missing commutative diagram}

A strong native theorem would require a construction of the form

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
X_k & \xrightarrow{T} & X_{k+1} \\
\downarrow \rho & & \downarrow \rho \\
H_k & \xrightarrow{L} & H_{k+1},
\end{array}
\]

where:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

\item \(T\) is an admitted native transport or loading law;

\item \(\rho\) is a declared registered readout;

\item \(L\) is a certified contrast-concentrating operator;

\item the diagram commutes;

\item some retained receipt or history distinction remains nontrivial
upstairs while registered contrast contracts downstairs.

\end{enumerate}

Paper 14 proves the required behavior for a candidate downstairs
operator,

\[
L=\widehat Q.
\]

It does not yet supply \(T\) and \(\rho\) with the required native
crosswalk.

\subsection{The role of global sigma}

The current inter-face sigma problem belongs exactly at this
provenance boundary.

A complete sigma would determine which locally admissible seam
relations coexist in one globally closed transport structure.

Only then could one ask whether repeated admissible transport induces
a contrast-concentrating registered sequence.

The current search has not established that global sigma.

Therefore the Mac result sharpens the theorem target without proving
saturation.

The appropriate reading is:

\begin{quote}
The Mac work tells us more precisely what would have to saturate. It
does not tell us that saturation occurs physically.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Long range as lawful sum}

The saturation conjecture also sharpens the meaning of long range in
the finite architecture.

No separate long-range selector is required by the current internal
picture.

Instead,

\[
\text{local admissible relation}
\]

contributes through

\[
\text{global compatibility}
\]

to

\[
\text{an admitted summation domain}.
\]

The global registered result is then obtained by lawful accumulation.

The exact incidence quadratic already provides one finite example of
such accumulation:

\[
x
\longmapsto
M^{\mathsf T}x
\longmapsto
MM^{\mathsf T}x.
\]

The regional cochain laws and history winding provide other additive
examples on different mathematical domains.

No universal identification among them is currently proved.

\subsection{Why finite capacity is still conjectural}

The contrast theorem does not by itself prove a universal finite
capacity of independent distinction.

The state space used in the theorem is finite-dimensional, but the
real coefficients form a continuum.

Thus finite dimension is not the same statement as a finite number of
possible real-valued states.

A stronger saturation theorem therefore needs a declared notion of
independent distinction.

Possible choices include:

\begin{itemize}

\item cardinality on a finite state family;

\item linear rank on a declared subspace;

\item distinguishability under a finite-resolution register;

\item combinatorial rank in a finite independence structure.

\end{itemize}

Paper 14 does not silently identify these notions.

The finite independent distinction capacity appearing in the broader
conjecture remains an explicit additional hypothesis.

\subsection{Why this matters for the conjecture}

This refinement makes the conjecture harder to satisfy, but also
better defined.

The theorem does not say:

\begin{quote}
Enough applications of \(Q\) destroy information.
\end{quote}

It says:

\begin{quote}
Repeated normalized quadratic aggregation suppresses centered
registered contrast relative to the common mode while preserving
exact finite-stage linear distinction.
\end{quote}

The difference is essential.

The second statement is proved.

The first would depend on what is meant by information and which
registration is being used.

\subsection{Clean release remains conditional}

The clean-release hypothesis arises naturally once coherent aggregate
and relational contrast are separated.

A useful outward registration may require both:

\[
\text{coherent strength}
\]

and

\[
\text{surviving individuation}.
\]

If organization initially improves the first without destroying the
second, a clean-release regime may exist.

If further loading suppresses the second, overcompression begins.

Nothing in the quadratic theorem guarantees that a particular native
loading family has such an interior optimum.

The theorem only establishes the contrast-suppressing direction that
would eventually bound such a regime.

\subsection{No singularity is required internally}

The normalized quadratic dynamics studied algebraically here remain
finite at every stage.

There is no divergent local coefficient.

There is no divergent state norm forced by the normalized operator.

Instead, the limiting phenomenon is loss of relative individuation.

This provides a mathematically simpler internal endpoint:

\[
\text{many complete distinctions}
\]
may become
\[
\text{poorly resolved by a declared registration}.
\]

That is the meaning of relational degeneracy in Paper 14.

Whether an analogous mechanism replaces a physical singularity is a
separate hypothesis.

\subsection{Methodological posture}

The saturation program follows the same rule as the earlier finite
mechanics:

\begin{quote}
Do not protect the idea. Make the idea protect itself.
\end{quote}

For Paper 14, that means keeping the following failures possible.

The conjecture should fail if:

\begin{itemize}

\item no native loading law induces contrast concentration;

\item the required retained distinction disappears at the complete
level;

\item global transport compatibility cannot be established;

\item the proposed saturation signature is not distinguishable from
appropriate null structures;

\item a physical bridge does not reproduce independently measured
observables.

\end{itemize}

The point of the theorem is not to guarantee the larger conjecture.

It is to make one part of the conjecture exact enough that the
remaining parts can fail cleanly.
